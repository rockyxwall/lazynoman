## What Is Fantasy Simulator?

Chen Heng is an ordinary guy who gets a **Fantasy Simulator system** — a cheat that lets him simulate different lives across various worlds. Each simulation is a new identity, a new power system, and a new chance to grow stronger. The catch? He can only bring back skills and abilities, not items. It's a world-hopping journey through sorcerer academies, cultivation empires, divine weapon realms, and more.

The premise is simple: jump into a world, live a life, gain power, repeat. If you've read simulation novels before, you know the loop. The question is whether the execution holds up across 853 chapters.

## Story Arc List

1. **Chaotic Era** (Sorcerer World) — Ch. 2–7: A solid intro that establishes the simulation mechanic. Short but effective hook.
2. **Royal Road** (Sorcerer World) — Ch. 17–101: The first real meat of the story. Academy setting, power progression, solid worldbuilding. This is where the novel hooks you.
3. **Azure Heaven Realm** (Great Chen Empire) — Ch. 117–151: Our first cultivation world. Felt familiar in a good way. Chen Heng builds an empire and it's satisfying.
4. **Sorcerer World** (Dawn Empire) — Ch. 161–214: Returning to the sorcerer setting with more political weight. Decent but I wanted more cultivation.
5. **Azure Heaven Realm** (Flowing Cloud Sect) — Ch. 216–256: Back to cultivation. This arc reminded me why I liked the novel.
6. **Mutated Creature World** — Ch. 286–314: Weird detour. Not bad, just... different. The mutation powers felt chaotic.
7. **World of Gods** (Kobold Kingdom) — Ch. 316: Very short. Felt like a filler simulation.
8. **Curse World** — Ch. 334–369: Interesting concept but the curse-based power system didn't click for me.
9. **World of Gods** (Karkoen Empire) — Ch. 406: Again felt like the author was avoiding new world creation.
10. **Hollow World** — Ch. 414–559: The longest arc and my least favorite. The power system here (fate/gods/beliefs) bored me. Almost dropped the novel here.
11. **Divine Weapon World** — Ch. 585–684: Started slow (MC becomes a sword? Really?) but actually got interesting. Time skips saved this arc.
12. **Primogenitor World** — Ch. 722–832: Bloodline-based world. Well-written but not my preference. Felt repetitive.
13. **World of Gods** — The End: Felt rushed. Ending shows a "start of a new journey" then just... stops. Unsatisfying.

## Story

The story is decent for the first 300 chapters. After that, it became a slog. I dropped it for months and only came back because I had nothing else to read. The issue is simple: I'm biased toward cultivation worlds, and the novel spends over 500 chapters avoiding them. The end reveal was interesting but not fully satisfying. The story quality varies wildly depending on which world Chen Heng lands in.

## Characters

All characters are irrelevant except Chen Heng, and even he isn't particularly memorable. There's zero character development after the first few arcs. Chen Heng is emotionally intelligent and makes smart decisions, but that's about it. Side characters come and go with each simulation. You won't remember any of them.

## Worldbuilding

Decent for a world-hopping novel, but inferior to _Comprehension Ability_ and _The Innkeeper_. Each world has its own power system and rules, but none of them feel fully fleshed out. The worlds exist as backdrops for Chen Heng to grind in, not as living, breathing settings.

## Pacing / Writing Style

Fast-paced with generous time skips and summaries — which is good. The first 300+ chapters flow smoothly. But around chapter 390, the writing style changes. Scenes start without proper context. Explanations pop up out of nowhere every few chapters. The translation also gets weird — pronoun inconsistencies ("she" vs "he" for the same person) and oddly phrased descriptions. It's readable, but the quality dip is noticeable.

## Unique Element

None. The simulation system is standard fare. The novel doesn't try anything new with the concept.

## Verdict & Recommendation

I won't actively recommend this to anyone. But if you want to waste time, the first 300 chapters are genuinely good. After that, your mileage will vary based on whether you like the worlds being visited.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Fantasy Simulator worth reading in 2026?

Only the first 300 chapters. After that, it depends entirely on whether you enjoy the specific worlds Chen Heng visits.

### How long is the novel? Is it finished?

853 chapters. Completed as of June 2026.

### Is there cultivation in this novel?

Yes, but it's not the main focus for most of the novel. The MC spends long stretches in non-cultivation worlds (gods, curses, divine weapons, bloodlines). Cultivation fans may be frustrated.

### What should I read after Fantasy Simulator?

If you want better world-hopping with a simulation system, try _Comprehension Ability_. For a more polished novel overall, _The Innkeeper_ is the better read.

### What's the ending like?

Unsatisfying. It ends with a "start of a new journey" scene that feels like the author ran out of steam. The extras are placed before the ending chronologically, which makes it worse.
