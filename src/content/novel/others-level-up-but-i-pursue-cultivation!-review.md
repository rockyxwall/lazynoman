## The Short Version

Solid simulator-cultivation novel. Great first half, repetitive middle, weak ending. Good chill read if you like OP MCs with zero suffering.

---

## My Review

I like this genre a lot, so take that into account — I was already going to enjoy this.

The premise is genuinely interesting. Su Xing can't level up like everyone else, so he cultivates instead. On top of that he gets a Life Simulator — he runs simulations of the future, dies in them, and brings back cultivation levels and items to the real world. It's a clever setup and the first half uses it well.

The middle gets repetitive. I was scrolling past chunks of it, but the story stayed interesting enough to keep going. The ending doesn't land. And the very end tries to explain how he got his powers at the beginning — and it absolutely does not make sense. That part left me confused more than satisfied.

On adult content — early on, the MC does have some physical moments with girls in his simulations. It fades out around chapter 300 and after that it's minimal. Maybe one or two real-world moments toward the end. Not that kind of novel, so don't go in expecting it.

I'd recommend this to people who like overpowered MCs, no real tension, no crisis, no suffering for the MC. Pure power fantasy with a system twist.

## Read or Skip?

**Read if —** you like simulator or cultivation novels, OP MCs with no real struggle, slow farming and development stories, chill reads.

**Skip if —** you need a strong ending, consistent translation, a memorable MC, or any real tension.

---

## What Others Think

Most readers agree the first half is the strongest part. The simulator concept pulls people in, the pacing is steady without being rushed, and the world building holds up. Several readers put it in their top ten cultivation reads based on the early experience alone.

The translation is the most consistent complaint. Numbers are wrong constantly — units swap, 100 million gets skipped entirely, 5 billion shows up where 500 million should be. Readable, but distracting if you pay attention to numbers at all. The story itself is strong enough that most readers say ignore it and move on.

After chapter 1300 the consensus shifts. Multiple readers describe the same thing — the author seemed to get tired, and the final stretch becomes slow and monotonous just to wrap things up. The MC is also noted as being a bit hollow — more of a vehicle for the system than a real character, which works fine if you're not looking for depth but disappoints readers who wanted more personality.

**Great concept, good first half, forgettable MC, rough ending. Worth trying if the genre fits you.**
