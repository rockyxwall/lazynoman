## Is It Worth Reading?

**Our Score: 7.5 / 10**

There's a very specific kind of novel that Chinese web fiction has been quietly perfecting — the simulation novel. The format is elegant: the protagonist gets a "try before you die" system, runs through simulated lives, harvests the results, and uses them to power up in the real world. *Simulation Towards Immortality In A Group Chat* takes that formula and wraps it inside a second hook: a transmigrators' group chat, where members from across different worlds compare notes, gloat, and watch each other die in real time.

It's a clever premise. Whether the execution lives up to it is a more complicated answer.

## The Hook — Genuinely Strong

The opening chapters are among the most effective in the simulation sub-genre. Wang Ping joins what looks like a golden ticket — a group chat that grants transmigrators exclusive cheats. Then, within moments of joining, 60 of the 67 members go offline. One of them, Chen Kang, dies within ten seconds of arriving in his world.

This is the novel's smartest move: it front-loads the danger, establishes the stakes instantly, and makes the group chat feel less like a power fantasy and more like a death lottery. Wang Ping doesn't feel like a chosen one — he feels like a survivor who got lucky in the talent draw. The Life Simulator he awakens feels like a natural, earned response to a world where one wrong step kills you permanently.

For the first hundred chapters, this tension holds. The group chat functions as a real-time news feed of doomed souls, and Wang Ping's careful, methodical approach to using simulations before committing feels genuinely smart.

## The Simulation System — A Double-Edged Sword

The Life Simulator is the novel's strongest original idea and also its biggest structural weakness.

**What works:** The simulator creates a satisfying loop. Wang Ping runs a life, makes mistakes, dies in the simulation, learns, resets, and comes back with new talents, techniques, or knowledge. The talent draws — which can produce ordinary skills or legendary ones — give each run a lottery-like excitement. Early on, the author makes each simulated life feel like it has real weight, even knowing Wang Ping will be resetting.

**What doesn't:** As the novel progresses, the simulations stop being *experienced* and start being *summarized*. Rather than living through Wang Ping's simulated lives chapter by chapter, the novel increasingly skips the runs to deliver the reward. The simulator quietly becomes a vending machine. You stop caring about the process and just wait for the ding.

This is a meaningful loss. Simulation novels work best when the simulated lives are themselves interesting — when you feel the accumulation of knowledge and the bittersweet cost of each reset. This novel delivers the dopamine without the emotional investment that should precede it.

## Wang Ping — A Protagonist Who Mostly Works

Wang Ping is, for the most part, a likable lead. He is cautious without being passive, clever without being smug, and his awareness of his own mortality keeps him grounded in ways most cultivation protagonists aren't. Readers tired of arrogant, face-slapping young masters will find him a welcome change.

That said, he isn't immune to the genre's worst habits. In the middle arcs he falls into the same patterns he criticizes in his simulated counterparts: accumulating power for its own sake, dismissing threats too easily, treating side characters as tools. Whether this is intentional character commentary or just the author sliding into default mode is hard to say.

The group chat members are more interesting as a concept than as actual characters. A few of them get real development, and the contrast between Wang Ping's careful simulator-backed approach and other members' reckless styles creates some of the novel's best moments. But most of them exist to die, provide exposition, or be impressed — and the chat fades in importance as the story goes on.

## Pacing and the Chapter-Splitting Problem

A practical note for new readers: the novel on Webnovel has a well-known issue where longer original chapters are split into multiple purchasable parts. A single chapter in the Chinese original can become three or four paid segments on Webnovel, creating an impression of padding and slow pacing that isn't entirely the author's fault.

If you're reading on Webnovel and chapters feel thin, this is likely why. Reading on an aggregator site with the original chapter structure will give you a noticeably better experience.

Even accounting for this, the middle section (roughly chapters 300–700) loses momentum. The worlds Wang Ping visits begin to feel repetitive — arrive, assess, simulate, dominate, leave — and the group chat loses the existential dread that made the opening compelling. The novel becomes comfortable in a way that blunts its own edge.

## The Ending

The ending has divided readers. The final arc moves quickly, and some feel it wraps up too fast given the buildup. Others appreciate that the author actually finished the story rather than dragging it out indefinitely — a genuine rarity in web fiction at this length.

The final chapter title, *Omniscient and Omnipotent*, tells you exactly where Wang Ping ends up. If you're here for the journey rather than a twist, you'll likely be satisfied. If you need the ending to interrogate the implications of the simulation premise — what it means to erase and rewrite a life repeatedly — you'll leave a little disappointed. The novel reaches its power fantasy destination without asking too many hard questions about what it cost to get there.

## Quick Verdict

| | |
|---|---|
| **Story hook** | ✅ One of the better simulation setups in the genre |
| **Protagonist** | ✅ Thoughtful and grounded, mostly |
| **Simulation depth** | ⚠️ Strong early, weakens mid-novel |
| **Group chat** | ⚠️ Great concept, underused later |
| **Pacing** | ⚠️ Repetitive in the middle arcs |
| **Completion** | ✅ Fully completed at 1,294 chapters |
| **Translation** | ⚠️ Chapter splitting on Webnovel is an issue |

**Read it if you** enjoy simulation/regression novels, want a cautious methodical MC, or need something completed that isn't a slog to finish.

**Skip it if you** need a simulation novel that takes the format seriously throughout the whole run — in that case, try *Immortality Simulator* instead.

**Final Score: 7.5 / 10** — A novel that starts stronger than it finishes, but a better-than-average entry in the genre that earns its readership.
